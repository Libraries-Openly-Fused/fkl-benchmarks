# E2: the element-wise chain through FKL, Triton and TorchInductor (RTX 4090, CUDA 13.4.3, Triton 3.8.0, PyTorch 2.14.1)

Workload: a 4,096 x 2,160 uint8 image converted to float32 and scaled by alpha = 1.0, then k operations alternating Mul by c and Add c (c = 0.54, k / 2 pairs), written as float32.
Every implementation reads the same bytes: `e2_input.u8` (std::mt19937_64 seed 20261030, uniform 0..255, as E1a; sha256 `726b9ef38a8277d6021009d344446bf5915fe75f746d07950e608f63614a8995`), written by
`handwritten-cuda/e2_fkl_chain.cu`, which also runs the FKL chain (thread fusion off, 32 x 8 threads per block) and writes one float32 output per k. `triton/e2_chain.py` runs Triton and TorchInductor on that
image, each (version, k) in its own process with fresh Triton and Inductor caches, and compares every output with FKL's and with an exact float64 evaluation. The image and the outputs (6 x 35 MB) stay in
`/home/oscar/tfkl-bench/e2-work`, not in the repository. k = 2, 10, 50, 100, 500 (`e2_chain.csv`) and the bounded larger attempt k = 5,002 with a 900 s limit per point (`e2_chain_large.csv`). `e2_compact.csv` is
the table for the paper (k = 10, 100, 500, 5,002).

Versions: FKL; `triton_hand` (one hand-written Triton kernel, the chain unrolled at trace time); `eager` (PyTorch, one kernel per operation); `inductor_default` (torch.compile, fusion window 64);
`inductor_wide` (window raised so that the whole chain may fuse); `inductor_graphs` (torch.compile with mode "reduce-overhead", CUDA Graphs); `eager_graphs` (eager captured in one CUDA graph by hand).

## Results
- **Agreement.** FKL, the hand Triton kernel and all three Inductor variants give bit-identical outputs at every k (bitwise fraction 1.0000, 0 ULP). Eager differs: 79 % of the elements are identical to FKL's at k = 2, 37 % at k = 10, 62 % at k = 50 and 99 % at k = 100, 500 and 5,002, with a maximum difference of 1 to 4 ULP (float32 ULPs of FKL's value). Against an exact float64 evaluation of the chain all of them are within 1.12 to 4.76 ULP (4.6 ULP for FKL at k = 10, 1.65 ULP for k >= 100). The eager differences fade with k because the chain converges to its fixed point (c / (1 - c) = 1.174), where the rounding of the individual operations stops mattering.
- **Contraction of Mul and Add (from the kernels).** `triton_hand`: the SASS of its kernel has one FFMA per Mul+Add pair per element and no FADD (columns `sass_ffma`, `sass_fadd`: 8 FFMA per pair for the 8 elements a thread handles, 8 FMUL for the scale by alpha; k = 500: 2,000 FFMA, 8 FMUL, 0 FADD). TorchInductor (the three variants): all the operations are contracted, FFMA only: 5 k FFMA, 0 FMUL, 0 FADD (no FMUL appears at all, so the scale by 1.0 is gone as well). FKL (`e1-vf-chain-v2/table.csv`, the same chain): k / 2 FFMA per element (51 at 102 operations, 501 at 1,002, 9,951 at 19,902), 1 FMUL for the scale and 0 FADD: one FFMA per pair, like Triton. PyTorch eager launches one kernel per operation (k + 2 kernels), so nothing can be contracted across operations: it rounds after every Mul and every Add, which is the whole difference with the others.
- **Kernels launched per call:** 1 for FKL, Triton and all Inductor variants (also at k = 5,002: Inductor fuses a pointwise chain into one kernel whatever the fusion window, so the default and the wide window give the same kernel); k + 2 for eager (4, 12, 52, 102, 502, 5,004).
- **GPU time (ms, mean of 100 calls):** k = 10: FKL 0.0213, Triton 0.0288, Inductor 0.0448; k = 100: 0.0266, 0.0290, 0.0463; k = 500: 0.0673, 0.0708, 0.0884; k = 5,002: FKL 0.545, Inductor 0.568, Triton 0.795. Eager: 0.363 (k = 10), 2.92 (100), 14.2 (500), 140.9 (5,002), launch-bound (host time of one call 2.6 ms at k = 100). Inductor with CUDA Graphs is slower than without at small k (0.057 against 0.045 ms at k = 10).
  These are warm timings: the 8.8 MB image and the 35 MB output fit the 72 MB L2 of the 4090 (FKL's 0.021 ms at k = 2 is below the 0.044 ms that reading and writing 44 MB from DRAM would take), the same as E1a.
- **Cold compile time (s)** is not the same operation in each column: FKL is an nvcc compile of the translation unit with the chain (object file, one core, `e2_fkl_compile.csv`: 3.65 s for k = 2 ... 100, 3.72 s at 500, 5.23 s at 5,002); Triton is the first call in a fresh process (Triton compile and ptxas: 0.7 to 0.9 s, 5.0 s at 5,002); Inductor is the first `torch.compile` call (1.2 s at k = 2 to 3.2 s at k = 500, 25.6 s at 5,002). Python start-up and `import torch` are outside all of them.
- **Larger attempt (k = 5,002):** neither Triton nor Inductor failed or timed out (900 s limit): the limit that FKL has with distinct operations (E3) is not reached by these tools with this chain, which they unroll into one kernel with the constants folded.
