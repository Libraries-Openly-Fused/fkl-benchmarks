# Test (i): the fold `cxp::max(...)` over BATCH arguments at -O0 (SYNTHETIC: one function of FKL 1f33224 copied as text into a test program; NOT a build of the 2025 library)

**What it is.** `fold_synth.cpp` copies, as text, `cxp::max` and its helper `internal::max_helper` from `include/fused_kernel/core/constexpr_libs/constexpr_cmath.cuh` at FKL 1f33224 (lines 49-76; only the macro `FK_HOST_DEVICE_CNST` is defined as `constexpr` and the static_assert uses `std::is_same`) and calls it the way `BatchRead<..., CONDITIONAL_WITH_DEFAULT>::build_helper` does (`batch_operations.cuh:255-256` at 1f33224): `cxp::max(Op::num_elems_x(Point(0u, 0u, 0u), instantiableOperations[Idx])...)` for the width and for the height, over B arguments. The per-argument call is a stand-in: a function returning the destination width of a 48-byte operation data that the instantiable operation derives from (so the argument binds without a copy, as at 1f33224, `instantiable_operations.cuh:64`). For comparison the loop form of the current library (`batch_operations.h:437-441` at c8fcbe8), with a two-argument stand-in for `cxp::max::f`. Nothing else of the 2025 library is in it; it is not the pre-LTS build that needs Oscar's exception (which was not asked of me and is not started). Host only: g++ 15.2, no CUDA. Five host flag sets: `none` (the flags nvcc passes to g++ with an empty CMAKE_BUILD_TYPE: no -O, no -DNDEBUG; see Test B), `-O0 -DNDEBUG`, `-O1 -DNDEBUG`, `-O2 -DNDEBUG`, `-O3 -DNDEBUG`. Pinned to cores 0-3,16-19, 3 rounds, median of 7 blocks per cell; load before 0.14 0.33 0.65, after 0.56 0.42 0.66 (`testI_results.csv`, `build-and-run.sh`). Written at the request of the coordinator (the criterion was set before the run: 'if the fold at -O0 adds about 0.04 us per crop above batch 42, it would account for the remaining factor of about 1.4 to 1.5').

**At -O0 (the empty build type) the fold bends upward:** its cost per crop is 0.022 us between B = 2 and 42 and 0.062 us between 42 and 142 (0.078 between 142 and 152), i.e. 0.040 us per crop more above batch 42 than below it; the loop form of the current library stays at 0.010 us per crop at every batch (so the fold costs 0.052 us per crop more than the loop above batch 42; at B = 142 7.1 us against 1.4 us, at B = 152 7.9 against 1.5). With optimisation (-O1 and above) both forms are flat and negligible (under 0.1 us at B = 152): the compiler flattens the recursion. So the fold is a cost of the unoptimised build only, and grows faster than linearly with the number of arguments.

The criterion set by the coordinator ('about 0.04 us per crop more above batch 42') is met by the numbers (0.040 against the fold's own slope below 42; 0.052 against the loop).
**What this shows and what not.** It shows that a function that the 2025 cvGS path calls (the fold of `cxp::max` over BATCH arguments in `BatchRead<CONDITIONAL_WITH_DEFAULT>::build_helper`, unconditional in that file, while the plain-policy variant that the FastNPP wrapper goes through has a loop branch when NDEBUG is not defined, `batch_operations.cuh:173-185`) has, copied as text and compiled at -O0, a cost that bends upward by about the amount needed, and that the current library does not have it (it uses loops). It does not show that the 2025 binaries were built without optimisation, nor that this is the only difference of the 2025 library code; the stand-in for the per-argument call, the real arguments (temporary conversions at -O0) and everything else of the 2025 `build_helper` are not in the test. No cause is claimed.
